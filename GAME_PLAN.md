# Pocket Workshop — Game Plan

## Game pitch

Run a small repair shop where an endless line of customers bring curious objects with quick, tactile puzzles to solve. Successful repairs earn coins, and puzzle variations grow harder as the shift continues. Players can drop in for a few minutes and keep going as long as they like.

## Design goals

- Easy to understand in the first minute.
- Repairs feel hands-on and satisfying on a touchscreen.
- Each repair is short, with a clear finish and reward; finishing it brings in the next customer.
- Progress is friendly and steady; no energy timers or pay-to-win upgrades.
- Portrait-first interface that works on phones and tablets.

## Core loop

1. Choose the next customer job.
2. Inspect the object and its simple repair request.
3. Complete a touch puzzle using the right tool or action.
4. See the repair result and earn coins.
5. Welcome the next customer and continue the shift.

## Current playable version

- One workshop screen with a short first-job hint.
- 3 repair puzzle types: clean/restore, connect or fit parts, and align/adjust.
- Endless queue of procedurally generated repair jobs using three puzzle mechanics.
- A growing spot-cleaning challenge, up to six wire pairs, and changing gear targets.
- Increasing difficulty and coin rewards across an ongoing shift.
- Local save for completed repairs, current job, coins, and sound setting.
- Mobile-first web app that can be installed on Android and iOS home screens and cached for offline play.

### Future additions

Decorations, more puzzle mechanics, customer stories, accounts, online visits, and live events can be added after the endless core feels good.

## Example repair variations

1. Clean a dusty object by rubbing the screen.
2. Reconnect a broken radio by matching colored wire ends.
3. Set a clock gear by turning it to the marked angle.
4. Clean another customer's object with more spots.
5. Reconnect a device with more colored wires.

The repair types rotate, object themes vary, and later jobs increase the number of spots or wire pairs and change the target gear angle.

## Progression and rewards

- Coins are earned for each completed repair, with a small reward increase over time.
- A new customer arrives after every successful repair; there is no final job.
- The current job number, completed repair count, and coins persist between sessions.
- Avoid punitive failure: let players retry immediately, with a gentle hint after repeated attempts.

## Monetization direction

Keep the core game playable without payment. If ads are added later, use optional rewarded ads for a hint or bonus coins, and consider a one-time purchase to remove ads. Do not interrupt a repair with an ad. Validate platform store and child-safety requirements before release.

## Milestones

1. **Endless core:** Keep expanding object themes and balancing how quickly difficulty grows.
2. **Progression:** Add optional shop upgrades and decoration goals for earned coins.
3. **Content:** Add more distinct repair interactions, customer personalities, sound, and localization.
4. **Release prep:** Check device layouts, performance, privacy/store requirements, and prepare store assets and builds.

## Decisions to make next

- Art direction: cozy hand-drawn, clean 3D, or bright flat 2D.
- First audience: general casual players or family-friendly play for younger players.
- Whether to include optional ads at launch or first release a paid/ad-free prototype.
- Game engine and target device range.

## Early success checks

- A new player can finish the first repair using the on-screen hint.
- Players understand what to do on the next job.
- Repair variations stay satisfying as the shift continues.
- The game stays readable and responsive on a small phone screen.
